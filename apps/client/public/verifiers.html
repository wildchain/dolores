For verifiers

Run a verifier. Get paid to check the work.

Every agent signs a mandate before it acts — the venues it may touch, the size caps, the slippage it may accept. Verifiers check each action against that mandate and attest the result on-chain. Post a bond in $DOL to qualify, and earn USDC for the work you do.

The job

What a verifier actually does.

Most of it is machine work. The mandate is code, so checking an action against it is a comparison, not an opinion. You only exercise judgment when a mandate is genuinely ambiguous — and that is rare by design.

01 / WATCH

Stream the actions

Your node subscribes to the agents you cover. Every action arrives alongside the mandate it was taken under.

02 / CHECK

Compare to the mandate

The signed policy is code. In almost every case compliance is a boolean your node settles on its own, in milliseconds.

03 / ATTEST

Sign the result

You write the attestation on-chain. It becomes a permanent part of that agent's record, and yours.

04 / SETTLE

Handle challenges

When an outcome is disputed and the mandate is unclear, verifiers convene as a bonded panel and settle it.

Verifier tiers

Work flows to the reliable.

Your record is public: uptime, attestations signed, challenges lost. The protocol routes more work to the verifiers with the strongest record, and pays accordingly. Everyone starts at the bottom.

Tier Your record Work allocation
Exemplary 850 - 1000 1.4x weight
Established 650 - 849 1.0x base weight
Developing 400 - 649 0.6x weight
Provisional 150 - 399 Shadow mode
Unverified 0 - 149 Not eligible

Post a bond to qualify

You bond $DOL worth roughly 30% of the agent bonds you cover. That bond is what makes your attestation worth anything — attest falsely and you forfeit it.

Why the bond is in $DOL, not USDC

Your bond has to lose value if the network is harmed, or wrecking it costs you nothing. Agent bonds stay in USDC because they compensate a specific loss. Different jobs, different assets.

Paid in USDC, for work done

Your compensation comes out of the fees agents pay to be verified — not from new tokens being printed. You earn for checking, not for waiting.

Others can back your bond

Once you have a record, delegators can add $DOL to your bond and take a share of your fees. More bond means more work you can take on. Their capital is at risk alongside yours.

Requirements

What it takes to run one.

Less than you would expect. Verification is I/O bound, not compute bound — there is no model to serve and nothing to train.

A machine that stays up.

A small always-on VPS and Solana RPC access. Modest CPU, no GPU. Uptime matters far more than horsepower.

A bond you can post.

Sized to the work you take on, not a fixed floor. Start with a handful of agents and scale the bond as your record builds.

Patience for the first month.

New verifiers run in shadow mode: you attest, we compare, nothing is at risk and nothing is paid. Clear it and you start earning.
